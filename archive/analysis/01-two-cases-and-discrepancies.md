# Working note 01 — two cases, and where the numbers stop agreeing

**Status: provisional.** Everything below comes from web-search result
summaries. No court document, no agency page, and no news article has been
retrieved — the container's egress policy refused every host on 2026-10-22.
Treat every figure as *reported*, not *established*. The purpose of this note
is to fix the questions before the documents arrive, not to answer them.

## 1. There are two cases, not one

New Mexico has two separate actions against Meta. They have different
charged conduct, different dockets, different juries, different
judgments, and different numbers. Coverage blends them constantly.

| | **Case A — data privacy** | **Case B — child safety** |
|---|---|---|
| Filed | 2021, AG Hector Balderas | 2023-12-05, AG Raúl Torrez |
| Docket | **not yet confirmed** | D-101-CV-2023-02838 |
| Subject | Cambridge Analytica; statements about data access, hate speech, misinformation | child exploitation and minors' mental health |
| Jury | 2026-09-25 — 26 of 29 statements misleading; ~43–44M violations | March 2026 — 37,500 willful violations at $5,000 each = $375M |
| Judgment | **2026-10-21 — $25B**, Judge Francis Mathew | 2026-08-06 — +$567M abatement fund; **$942M total** |
| Posture | Meta appealing | Meta appealing |

Both sit in the First Judicial District Court, County of Santa Fe. Both run on
the same statute, the New Mexico Unfair Practices Act, with its $5,000
per-violation maximum. That shared machinery is probably why the two collapse
into each other so easily in secondary coverage.

**Observed conflation.** A search summary drawing on Albuquerque Journal
material attributed Case B's March $375M jury verdict to Case A. A separate
summary set Case A's $25B judgment beside "the AG's office describes a final
judgment of $567 million" — Case B's abatement figure — as though the two
described one outcome. A third mixes in a $953M figure from a Santa Fe New
Mexican headline about what the AG asked for, which is an *ask*, not an award.

## 3. Omission to check in the Fox Business piece

Multiple outlets report that Judge Mathew found misrepresentations made
*personally* by Meta's most senior leadership — naming Zuckerberg and Sandberg
— and characterised the conduct as a deliberate strategy. The Fox Business
article quotes Mathew's "goes to the heart of Facebook's core business
practices" line but does not mention the findings about the two executives.

Whether that is an omission worth weight depends on the order's own emphasis,
which requires the order. Flagged, not concluded.

Structurally, the Fox piece gives its last ~250 words to a defence-side
litigation partner whose quoted position is that Meta will not pay anything
close to $25B, and runs Meta's "tax on speech" framing at length. The state's
side is two sentences from Torrez. That is a real asymmetry in a news article,
but a single article's balance proves little on its own — the question is
whether it holds across the outlet's coverage, which is a corpus question.

## 4. Source-quality problem

Several domains ranking highly for this story — `tech-insider.org`,
`custommapposter.com`, `de-amplify.com`, `news.symplexia.com`, `lawsnap.com` —
have the signature of generated aggregation: no byline, no court documents, no
original reporting, confident restatement of figures that trace back to one or
two wire stories. They are in the retrieval list, tagged low-trust, because
how a wrong number propagates is part of what we are studying.

## 5. What the documents need to settle

1. Case A's docket number and full procedural history.
2. The exact violation count and the arithmetic from count to $219.5B to $25B.
3. Mathew's stated grounds for the reduction, and whether due process is addressed.
4. Whether the order names Zuckerberg and Sandberg, and in what terms.
5. The post-judgment interest rate, from the judgment itself.
6. Meta's accrual language in its SEC filings, against its public statements.
7. The state's actual penalty request, from its brief.
